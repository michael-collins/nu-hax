## Changes to the spec

For WP-04, WP-05 and WP-11, in order of consequence. Points 1 to 12 came from Chrome and hold in Firefox and WebKit unless they say otherwise; 13 to 22 come from Firefox and WebKit.

1. **§3.3 Save, step 3: don't end editing on HAX's answer.** `store.activeItemContent` is not current when the answer arrives. 300 ms later HAX starts its reload: it resets `store.activeId`, which redraws the reading copy from its cached `__pageContent` (the *old* page), then fetches the page and `site.json`, and redraws the reading copy 3 or 4 more times. Each redraw empties the theme's light DOM and refills it 5 ms later. Ending editing on the answer would show exactly what stock Save shows. The prototype waits for `haxcms-trigger-update-node`, then until no `loadPageData`, `loadJOSData` or `_activeItemContentChanged` is running, `haxcms-site-builder.loading` and `__pendingPageLoad` are false, and 300 ms have passed with no redraw (capped at 10 s; see point 18). That's 3.7 s after the answer in Chrome, 7.1 s in Firefox and 4.3 s in WebKit (headless; the 300 ms timer and the redraws run late on a busy main thread).
2. **While holding, block HAX's imports into hax-body.** HAX imports the reloaded page into the editor too, 4 or 5 times per save in all three: the site editor's edit-mode autorun when `activeItemContent` changes, and its `_bodyChanged` on every `json-outline-schema-active-body-changed`. Each import empties hax-body and refills it in a `setTimeout`, so the editor goes blank for a frame (seen with `keepEditMode`). The prototype wraps `body.importContent` while saving and releases it when editing ends.
3. **A late answer after the timeout.** Keep listening after the 20 s timeout. If the answer comes, the page was saved: the baseline becomes what was sent, the status follows from it, and HAX's reload must not reach the editor. Without the hold, it replaced " and more", typed after the timeout, with the saved copy.
4. **`inert` drops focus to `<body>`.** Keep the caret's range before setting `inert` and put it back when a save fails (the prototype's `keepFocus`), with `setBaseAndExtent()` (point 13).
5. **§3.4 and S6: every outline save re-imports the page while editing.** Each manifest reload redraws the reading copy, and the site editor's `_bodyChanged` imports it into hax-body unconditionally (a Page details save of the course sometimes brought two more imports in Firefox and every time in WebKit, not from `_bodyChanged`). Typed text is lost and one undo step is added. This is true for Style, for a course page field and for the site's own fields, in all three engines. WP-11 holds imports around `saveOutline` while editing, as in point 2. The spec's snapshot-and-re-import fallback isn't needed.
6. **New: a page save after a mid-edit Page details save undoes it.** The page-break in hax-body still carries the title, description and other details from when editing began, and HAXcms writes them back to `site.json` with the page: the description went back to "Digital Fabrication Studio, 4 credits." (every run in Chrome and WebKit, three of four in Firefox). The prototype (`freshDetails`) rewrites those attributes at save time from the page-break HAX built from the current manifest. It's registered before the theme's `__beforeSave`, so it runs inside it, and the theme's order fix still holds (stored order 14 → 14). In WP-11 it belongs in `__beforeSave` itself.
7. **§6.2 #2, the guard needs three more rules:**
   - **Refuse a deletion whose target range is collapsed.** Chrome reports a collapsed range at a block's edge. Backspace in the hub's empty intro paragraph emptied hax-body entirely, page-break included (3 → 0 children). Firefox and WebKit never reported a collapsed range for a deletion: at a block's edge their range reaches back into the block before, which "spans two blocks" refuses (point 20). Ordinary deletions inside text (Backspace, Delete, ⌥Backspace) have a range that isn't collapsed in all three, so the rule never catches them.
   - **Collapse a refused selection on `compositionstart`.** Composition input (`insertCompositionText`) is not cancelable in Chrome, and composing over a selection across sections removed a section (10 → 9 blocks). Collapsing first kept every block. This was emulated with CDP `Input.imeSetComposition`. Firefox and WebKit can't be driven through an IME headless, so there a synthetic `compositionstart` was dispatched over the same selection: the listener collapsed it in both, and the text then typed went in at the caret. Check a real IME by hand in all three.
   - **Handle paste before HAX.** HAX's window `paste` handler cancels the paste and edits the DOM itself: `range.deleteContents()`, `execCommand("insertParagraph")`, `haxReplaceNode`. So no `beforeinput` follows. A capture `paste` listener on hax-body collapses a refused selection, and in list items and headings inside a section it inserts plain text on one line (`execCommand("insertText")`) and stops HAX. Pasting two HTML paragraphs with the caret in a Tools item **replaced the hero** with two `<li>`s in Chrome (stock, every time). Firefox and WebKit damaged the list instead (point 15). The listener kept every block in all three.
8. **Not fixed here, for WP-04 or WP-14:** on any page, HAX's paste of two or more HTML paragraphs in the middle of a paragraph loses the text before the caret and inserts the paragraphs in reverse order. Pasting "First / Second" at offset 3 of "A note from…" gave "Second", "First", "ote from…" (Chrome probe, synthetic paste event; Firefox does something else again, point 15).
9. **§6.2 #3: normalize `element-visible` too.** page-break (and other IntersectionObserver blocks) toggles it as it scrolls in and out. A click that scrolled the page became an undo step and "Unsaved changes" until it was added.
10. **§3.3, the baseline: don't wait on `isContentBusy()`.** In 26.8.1, hax-body's `updated()` sets `editModeTransitioning`, then `waitForStable()` waits for every flag that's set, itself included, so it never clears. The content state is busy for the whole session. The prototype waits for `importing` and `inserting` instead, then 150 ms and two frames. **Upstream bug candidate:** for the same reason `_connectMutationObserverWhenReady()` never returns, and hax-body's own MutationObserver never connects (`_observer` stayed `null` after two edit entries). Anything the spec takes from hax-body.js 4755–4826 (ids on inserted headings, activating inserted nodes) doesn't run in this build. WP-01's notes credit that observer; worth a second look there.
11. **Leave guard:** HAX sets `globalThis.onbeforeunload` while editing and asks even with no changes. In the source it's skipped when `HAXStore.skipExitTrap` is true, so WP-11 can set that from `dirty` (not tested here).
12. **Shift-click can't select across sections in Chrome, but can in Firefox and WebKit.** In Chrome HAX's `_mouseDown` makes the clicked block active and the selection collapses. In Firefox and WebKit, Shift-click from the instructor's note into Tools selected "e from the instructor. Lase", across two sections. Shift+arrows can in all three, so the guard is needed everywhere.
13. **WebKit: every selection read and write in the editor goes through composed ranges (WP-04 `boundary-guard.js`, WP-05 `editor-state.js`, WP-11 `stock.js`).** WebKit has no `ShadowRoot.getSelection()`, and its `document.getSelection()` stops at the outermost shadow host (`haxcms-site-builder`, offset 0) whatever is selected in hax-body. Firefox's `document.getSelection()` holds the nodes inside, so it needs nothing. The prototypes as first written for Chrome (`root.getSelection?.() || document.getSelection()`) did nothing in WebKit: the `compositionstart` rule found no selection in hax-body and left the selection across two sections as it was; the paste rule let HAX's paste through (it split the Tools item and dropped the pasted paragraphs, with page errors "null is not an object (evaluating 'r.head.appendChild')"); and after a failed save the caret wasn't put back (focus stayed on `<body>`). The prototype's `selectionIn(body)` reads `getComposedRanges({ shadowRoots })` (WebKit 18 also takes the roots as arguments) into a `Range` when the selection's nodes aren't in hax-body's root. Every write uses `collapse()` or `setBaseAndExtent()`, which take nodes inside shadow roots in all three; WebKit ignores `addRange()` with them, and `collapseToStart()` there would collapse to the host. With that, every S3, S4 and S5 claim passed in WebKit. WP-05's `selectionchange` sync must read the caret the same way, or it repeats point 14.
14. **WebKit: HAX's arrow-key handler takes the caret out of the content (WP-04 `hax-fixes.js`; upstream bug candidate).** hax-body's `_onKeyDown` (hax-body.js 1861–1900) handles every arrow key by reading `HAXStore.getRange()` in a `setTimeout` and activating the block that holds it. `getRange()` (hax-store.js 97–125) uses `root.getSelection()` when hax-body's root has it and `globalThis.getSelection()` otherwise, so in WebKit it gets the host, and `__focusLogic(haxcms-site-builder)` follows: after ArrowDown in a list, ArrowRight in a paragraph or ArrowDown out of Tools, the caret was in the theme's `article` at offset 4, focus on `<body>`, HAX's active node `haxcms-site-builder`, and hax-body no longer `contenteditable`. Typing then goes nowhere until the author clicks back in. Stock HAX has this today in Safari, assuming Safari behaves as Playwright's WebKit does. The prototype `installRange` wraps `getRange` on the HAXStore instance (fingerprint `getRangeAt(0)`) to return `selectionIn(activeHaxBody).range` when there is one; with it the caret stayed in the content in all three, and Shift+↓ reached the next block in WebKit too. HAX's Backspace and paste handlers, which also call `getRange()`, get the same fix. `getSelection()` itself still returns the document selection in WebKit, so its `toString()` and `isCollapsed` there describe the host. Pin `getRange` in WP-04 with the other fixes, and offer it upstream with a `getComposedRanges` branch in `getSelection()`.
15. **Firefox: a real ⌘V runs both HAX's paste and the browser's (WP-04).** Firefox gives a synthetic `ClipboardEvent` no data (`getData()` is `""`), so S3 pasted for real there: ⌘C of two paragraphs from outside the editor, then ⌘V. Stock, the browser's own paste (`insertFromPaste`, cancelable) ran as well as HAX's: in the HTML path HAX calls `preventDefault()` only after `await this.htmlToHaxElements()` (hax-store.js 2333, then 2366–2587), when the event has long been dispatched. Chrome and WebKit were only given synthetic events, which have no default action, so expect the same from a real ⌘V there. In the Tools item that left `<li>Fus<div></div></li><li>First pasted</li><li>Second pasted</li><p>Second pasted</p><li><div><p></p></div>ion 360</li>`; in the instructor's paragraph, a `div` holding "First pasted", "Second pasted" and "Second pasted" again. The guard's paste listener only takes list items and headings in a section, so the paragraph came out the same with the guard on. WP-04 must cancel the browser's paste whenever HAX handles one (`preventDefault()` on the `paste` event in the re-bound handler of §6.2 #1, synchronously, before HAX's first `await`), or it pastes twice. In WebKit (a synthetic event, whose data WebKit reads) HAX's paste split the item at the caret (`insertParagraph`) and the pasted paragraphs didn't arrive.
16. **⌘Z in Firefox and WebKit is the browser's own undo (WP-04 `undo.js`).** Stock, ⌘Z there removed the typed text natively (`historyUndo`, cancelable, not prevented) and HAX recorded that as a *new* undo step, as the Edit menu's Undo did in Chrome. The keydown capture prevents it in both and calls `body.undo()`. With the keydown capture switched off, the `historyUndo` route caught the browser's undo instead (0 → −1, no new step). Both halves are needed: the keydown for the key, `beforeinput` for the Edit menu.
17. **The baseline is what makes Exit honest in Firefox and WebKit (WP-05, WP-11).** HAX's import into hax-body lands up to 700 ms after edit mode starts there, after HAX's own 100 ms "unchanged" copy, which is why stock Cancel asks "Leave without saving?" with nothing changed (the harness's smoke check fails on it in both). The prototype baseline waits for `importing`, and read "No changes" on entry, after clicking five blocks and after undoing back to the start, in all three. WP-11's Exit and leave guard must use it, never HAX's copy or its `onbeforeunload`.
18. **The hold's 10 s cap is too short (WP-11 `stock.js`).** HAX's reload settled 8.1 s after Save in Firefox (7.1 s after its answer), 5.2 s in WebKit and 4.7 s in Chrome. In two earlier Firefox runs it took 8.9 s and 10.4 s after Save, 0.7 s short of the cap, which counts from the answer. When the cap runs out the prototype ends editing anyway, and HAX's remaining redraws show the old page: the flash this design removes. Use 20 s, and keep "Saving…" up rather than landing early. Stock Save is slower still: in Firefox the old tagline showed until 10.2 s and the new one hadn't appeared by 12.8 s.
19. **WebKit: HAX's own restore loses the recovered edits (WP-11 `recovery.js`; upstream bug candidate).** HAX applies `__pendingRestore` in a `setTimeout(0)` after starting its import on entering edit mode (haxcms-site-editor.js 57–72), and `importContent` refuses a second import while one is running ("Import already in progress, skipping duplicate call", hax-body.js 3563–3568). In WebKit the first import is still running, so the restore is skipped and `__pendingRestore` is cleared: HAX's toast had promised "Recovered unsaved edits… select Edit to restore them", and the edits were gone. Chrome and Firefox applied it (without asking). The prototype's path, which takes over `__pendingRestore` before HAX reads it and imports the copy after the baseline, restored them in all three.
20. **Edges in Firefox and WebKit reach into the block before (WP-04), and the WP-03 stopgap doesn't hold in WebKit.** With the stopgap switched off, Backspace at a block's edge gave ranges like these: in Firefox, at the start of the tagline, `page-break@0 → hero p@0` (stock removed the hero, 10 → 9 blocks); in an empty outcome, `oer-cs-facts@0 → oer-cs-learn@1` (stock removed the Learn section); in the hub's empty intro, `page-break@0 → intro@1` (stock removed the intro, 3 → 2). In WebKit an empty outcome gave the hero's `p@0 → li@0`, and stock again removed the Learn section. The guard refused every one as spanning two blocks. The stopgap (cs-shared.js `keepEdges`, a keydown capture) stops these in Chrome and Firefox before any `beforeinput`, but it reads the selection with `getRootNode().getSelection?.() || globalThis.getSelection()`, so in WebKit it never finds the caret: with it on and the guard off, Backspace at the start of a question still merged it into the paragraph before, Delete at the end of the last tool still pulled the next paragraph in, and Backspace in an empty outcome still removed the Learn section. WebKit also reports Delete over a selection as `deleteContentBackward`, not `Forward`; the guard treats both alike.
21. **Select All reaches every block (WP-04).** ⌘A in the content selected all of hax-body in Firefox (`hax-body@0 → hax-body@10`) and from the first text to the last in WebKit. Stock Backspace then **emptied hax-body, page-break included (10 → 0), in Firefox**, and left 3 blocks in WebKit. The guard refused both. In Chrome the `selectAll` editing command selected 237 characters and Backspace did nothing at all, guard or not; check ⌘A in a real Chrome window.
22. **Harness limits, not product behaviour.** WebKit's `InputEvent` constructor drops `targetRanges`, and WebKit keeps a selection set from script inside the block it starts in, so a synthetic drag across sections can't be described there (SKIP); Chrome's and Firefox's carried their ranges and were refused. Playwright's WebKit goes back a page on Backspace when the caret isn't in editable content, and it put no usable caret in the hub's empty intro paragraph, so that case is SKIP in WebKit.

Its log (input type, target ranges, what it judged by, reason) is in each row of the report.

**Manual checks:** a real IME (Japanese, Chinese, and macOS press-and-hold accents) over a selection across sections, in all three; dragging selected text across sections, in all three; ⌘A then Delete in a real Chrome window; a real ⌘V in Chrome and Safari (Firefox's was real here); and the arrow keys in a real Safari, to see point 14 outside Playwright's build.

## S4: undo and unsaved changes

| Check | Stock | Prototype (all three) |
|---|---|---|
| Undo steps after entering edit mode | 1 | **0** |
| … after clicking five blocks | 5 more | **0** (7 snapshots skipped as unchanged) |
| … after typing "abc" | 1 more | **1** |
| ⌘Z | Chrome: nothing (HAX listens for Ctrl+Z only). Firefox and WebKit: the browser's own undo, which HAX recorded as a new step | HAX's undo, position 0 → −1 |
| ⌘⇧Z | – | redo, −1 → 0 |
| The browser's Undo command (Edit menu; `historyUndo`, cancelable in all three) | the browser's own undo removed the text, and HAX recorded that as a *new* step | prevented and routed to HAX's undo, 0 → −1, no new step |
| Unsaved changes (entering, clicks, typing, undo, redo, undo) | – | false, false, true, false, true, false |

The stock step counts were the same in all three. The Edit menu's Undo can't be chosen headless: Chrome ran its `undo` editing command with no Z key; Firefox and WebKit, whose ⌘Z runs that same command, had ⌘Z pressed with the prototype's keydown capture switched off (`undoKeydown: false`), so the browser's undo reached `beforeinput`.

The wrapper keeps HAX's snapshots raw (so undo still restores `data-hax-active`). When the normalized HTML equals the normalized previous snapshot, it refreshes `undoStackPrevValue` and records nothing. The keys leave form fields outside hax-body alone (Page details, the image address); that part wasn't checked here.

## S5: saving

Fixture: the hero's tagline holds "Old tagline from before the save"; it's replaced by typing "New tagline typed just now". The sampler checks, every 50 ms and every animation frame, whether either tagline is visible anywhere in the theme (through shadow roots), and which view is up. The timings are in the table under the verdict.

| Check | Chrome | Firefox | WebKit |

Failure is detected by wrapping the site editor's `_handleNodeResponse` and `lastErrorChanged` for one save (401 and 403 first try a fresh sign-in, so they don't count as failure while there's a JWT). `saveNode` is bound at connect, so it can't be wrapped or awaited; dispatching `haxcms-save-node` keeps the theme's capture listener first. Failures were simulated with request interception (Playwright routes in Firefox and WebKit).

## S6: Style and Page details mid-edit, and recovery

Each case typed a marker into the tagline, then saved through the real `oer-site-style` and `oer-page-details` elements (`_save()`), and waited for HAX's manifest reload. The results were the same in all three engines except where noted.

| Check | Stock | Prototype (imports held) |

| Recovery check | Result |
|---|---|
| Unsaved edits copied within 2 s | `sessionStorage["haxcms-pending-edit"]` holds the page, through HAX's `_snapshotPendingEditForLogout()` from our debounce. HAX writes it by itself only when a session ends. |
| After a reload, HAX doesn't restore by itself | An accessor on the site editor's `__pendingRestore`, installed as soon as the editor exists, keeps the copy and reads `null` to HAX. HAX still toasts "Recovered unsaved edits to DART 413… select Edit to restore them": filter it in `quiet-mode.js`. |
